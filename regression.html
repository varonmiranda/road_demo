Part 2 · Satellite evidence: are these relationships real?

Historical, not live. Satellites only measure on clear passes (Landsat every 8–16 days, MODIS nightly at 1 km), so they can't run in real time. Part 2 checks the relationships Part 1 relies on against satellite measurements of 4,000 random DC roads.

1 · What predicts a cold road surface

Multiple regression of each satellite measurement on nine physical factors at once, so each bar shows that factor's effect holding the others fixed. Effects are per 1 standard deviation of the factor. Error bars are 95% confidence intervals with errors clustered by 1 km grid cell. Gray means the interval crosses zero: no reliable effect.

4 · How the factors move together

Spearman rank correlations. Watch for factors that travel together, like tree canopy vs impervious surface: the regression separates their effects, but the more two factors overlap, the wider the error bars.

5 · How well can these factors predict? (the honest test)

R² = share of road-to-road variation explained, measured on roads the model never saw. Random holds out random roads, whose neighbors were still used for fitting. Spatial holds out whole 2 km neighborhoods, which is how the model would face a new part of the city. A big gap means the model memorized places rather than learning physics.
